Character Filter — Goal and Examples Review

Review method
Vale → Skeptic → Professor → Continuity Auditor → Editor → Vale reconciliation.

Goal
The game teaches character voice through perception. The learner should see that voice is not limited to dialogue or slang: a character becomes distinct through what they notice, compare, value, fear, judge, ignore, and treat as important.

How the mechanic meets the goal
The game now has two modes.

Controlled comparison is the default. It keeps the neutral subject and one perceptual “voice move” fixed while three different character filters describe the same thing. That isolates the character filter, so the learner can compare how different values and assumptions change perception.

Challenge mode varies both the character filter and the voice move. It remains available as a harder exercise after the learner understands the controlled comparison.

The game also now states its scope explicitly: it teaches perceptual voice. Syntax, diction, rhythm, metaphor, and other prose-level choices contribute to full narrative voice but are outside this particular exercise.

How the examples support the goal
The bank contains 10 neutral subjects, 10 character filters, and 10 voice moves, producing far more than 30 coherent exercises.

The subjects are deliberately ordinary—a porch, lost-and-found table, cracked phone screen, hotel room, muddy boots, birthday cake, parking lot, family photograph, vending machine, and row of houses—so the mind looking at the object supplies the difference.

The filters represent distinct priorities and worldviews: distrust of perfection, nostalgia, efficiency, pessimism, delight in messiness, sensitivity to money, jealousy, status awareness, fear of judgment, and practical tool-oriented thinking.

The voice moves direct attention toward cost, risk, social interpretation, tiny flaws, usefulness, prior use, personal comparison, unfair judgment, and symbolic meaning.

In controlled mode, one voice move is held constant across all three characters. This makes it much easier to see how perception itself creates distinct narrative voice.

Review
The previous comparison problem has been corrected. Introductory play no longer changes both the character and perceptual technique at the same time. The harder compounded version remains available separately.

Verdict
PASS. The game now isolates the intended variable clearly while retaining a large example space and an explicit advanced mode.
