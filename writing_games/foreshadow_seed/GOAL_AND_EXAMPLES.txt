Foreshadow Seed — Goal and Examples Review

Review method
Vale → Skeptic → Professor → Continuity Auditor → Editor → Vale reconciliation.

Goal
The game teaches foreshadowing as delayed meaning. An early detail should have a believable reason to exist in the present scene, gain a little weight when echoed, and become newly meaningful when a later reveal occurs.

How the mechanic meets the goal
The game now uses 30 curated foreshadowing seeds rather than independently randomizing a reveal and a detail.

Each seed supplies three connected pieces:
1. a future reveal;
2. an ordinary detail that can support that reveal;
3. a present-scene reason for the detail to exist before it becomes important.

The learner then works through three stages:
PLANT — include the detail for its ordinary present purpose while attention is elsewhere.
ECHO — bring it back naturally without explaining what it will mean.
CONNECT — let the reveal make the earlier detail newly understandable without adding a new coincidence.

This structure keeps the actual writing work focused on subtle placement and payoff instead of forcing the learner to invent missing causal machinery just to make a random pair function.

How the examples support the goal
The 30 examples cover multiple kinds of foreshadowing: knowledge gaps, hidden objects, access failures, disguise, mistaken message identity, weather danger, intentional sabotage, planned departure, objects hidden in plain sight, and covert listening.

Each reveal has three different compatible details. Examples include a guide using a map folded along the wrong route; a room with a slow keycard reader before access fails; a familiar ring visible beneath a disguised person’s work glove; upstream rainfall mentioned before a road floods; a safety guard reset too neatly before sabotage is confirmed; and a chair turned toward a hallway before covert listening is revealed.

The examples pass the three standing tests:
- the detail has an ordinary reason to exist before the reveal;
- it can be echoed without announcing the answer;
- the reveal changes its meaning without requiring a fresh coincidence.

Review
The previous structural problem has been removed. The game no longer claims 100 arbitrary combinations as meaningful examples. It has 30 explicitly compatible reveal-detail-present-purpose seeds, and the learner is shown enough present context to avoid inventing operational logic that the exercise should have supplied.

Verdict
PASS. The mechanic, example bank, and continuity rules now support the stated foreshadowing goal directly.
