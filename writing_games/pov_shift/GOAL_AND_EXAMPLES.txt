POV Shift — Goal and Examples Review

Review method
Vale → Skeptic → Professor → Continuity Auditor → Editor → Vale reconciliation.

Goal
The game teaches that point of view changes a scene even when the external event does not change. Different minds notice different details, assign different meanings, omit different information, and form different conclusions.

How the mechanic meets the goal
The game now has two modes.

Controlled comparison is the default. It keeps the neutral event and one craft mission fixed while three different witnesses interpret the same situation. That means the major changing variable is point of view, so the learner can compare the results directly.

Challenge mode is deliberately harder. It keeps the event fixed but changes both the witness and the mission, creating a compounded exercise after the basic distinction is understood.

How the examples support the goal
The bank contains 10 neutral events, 10 viewpoint filters, and 10 craft missions, producing far more than 30 coherent exercises.

The events are open enough to support different interpretations without changing their facts: a sealed box left on a café table, a power outage, an unfamiliar glove carried home by a dog, a bus stopping early, a performer freezing during a show, and a family member arriving with an unexpected suitcase.

The viewpoint filters represent different priorities and assumptions: suspicious, optimistic, responsible, childlike, exhausted, secretive, rule-focused, thrill-seeking, grieving, and practical.

The missions target specific POV effects such as believable misreading, omission, selective urgency, personal comparison, power awareness, avoidance, mistaken conclusions, worldview-supporting details, and making identical facts feel safer or more threatening.

In controlled mode, one of those missions is held constant across all three witnesses. This makes differences in selection, interpretation, emphasis, and omission much easier to attribute to the viewpoint itself.

Review
The earlier confounding problem has been fixed. Introductory play no longer changes the viewpoint and craft instruction simultaneously. The more complex version remains available as an explicit challenge mode rather than being the only way to play.

Verdict
PASS. The mechanic now provides both a clean introductory comparison and a harder extension while retaining a large, meaningful example space.
