# Review notes

## Vale
The game is built around adapting a scene rather than merely reacting to a random prompt. The scene must provide enough concrete information for a player to begin immediately without inventing missing operational facts.

## Skeptic
The timer is tension, not the lesson. The learning depends on whether the ambush attacks an established assumption and forces a meaningful change. Avoid turning the story-state display into a fake quality score. Reject scenarios that rely on genre shorthand, unexplained procedures, convenient records, mysterious documents, or evidence whose meaning the player has to invent.

## Professor
The learning objective is causal adaptation under changed story conditions. The debrief requires the group to identify setup, changed conditions, and persistent consequence so the exercise transfers back to scene construction.

## Continuity Auditor
Continuity Auditor is the plot-hole and causal-logic reviewer. It tests the scenario before final editorial polish.

Responsibilities:

- track who knows what, when they learned it, and whether they could reasonably know it;
- track where important people, objects, resources, obligations, and risks were before the current scene;
- ask why the problem exists now rather than earlier;
- identify obvious problems that competent characters would reasonably have noticed, planned for, or resolved already;
- verify that prior plans were valid before a specific change caused them to fail;
- verify that every reveal has a believable source and timing;
- check timelines, travel time, access, injuries, resources, ownership, procedures, responsibilities, and physical possibility;
- distinguish deliberate mystery from missing authorial logic;
- reject conveniently timed information, coincidences, or irrational behaviour that exist mainly to force the plot forward.

Core questions:

- What had to be true before this scene for this to happen now?
- Who knew about it, and when?
- Why did they not deal with it earlier?
- What was the prior plan?
- What specifically changed?
- Does that change actually cause the new problem?
- Why is this happening now rather than five minutes earlier or later?

Preferred causal pattern:

**valid prior state/plan → specific change or discovery → old plan no longer works → new pressure, choice, or consequence**

Continuity Auditor may mark a scenario **Pass**, **Repair**, or **Reject**. Reject means the causal setup is unsound and should not proceed to final editorial review until repaired.

## Editor
Each scenario must contain an explicit goal, pressure, assumption, and causal ambush. Ambushes must be tied to prior facts rather than unrelated disasters.

### Evidence and context rule
If a scene depends on a record, log, signal, document, procedure, physical clue, expert conclusion, or other evidence, the player must understand:

1. who or what produced it;
2. what it normally tells the characters;
3. why its presence, absence, or change matters now.

A cold reader should not have to invent the missing explanation for the scenario to work. If the evidence would naturally raise “what record?”, “who made it?”, “why does that matter?”, or “how would they know that?”, the setup is incomplete and must be repaired before use.

### Human-logic rule
Information must reach characters through a believable source. Characters may be mistaken, deceptive, or uncertain, but the scenario must distinguish an established fact from an assumption, inference, rumour, or claim. A reveal should change the scene because of what it means to the characters, not because the writer withheld basic context from the player.

### Reason-for-now rule
Every important condition in the setup and every ambush must have a credible reason for existing **now**.

Do not create a dilemma by hiding an obvious problem from characters who reasonably would have noticed, planned for, or resolved it earlier. If competent people already knew about a constraint, the scenario must explain why their earlier plan was reasonable.

When an ambush creates a new problem, identify the causal change that made the old plan stop working. Useful causes include:

- a previously valid option is removed;
- a separate plan or route fails;
- new information changes the meaning of an existing fact;
- a resource becomes unavailable;
- responsibility changes hands;
- a deadline, rule, relationship, or external condition changes;
- something that was legitimately unknown becomes knowable.

The writer should be able to answer: **Why was this not already a problem five minutes ago?**

If the answer is only “the player did not know yet,” that is insufficient unless the characters also had a believable reason not to know.

### Prior-plan rule
When people, resources, passengers, obligations, or risks suddenly enter the active conflict, establish what was supposed to happen to them before the ambush.

For example, if extra evacuees suddenly need seats on a shuttle, the scenario should establish that they were assigned to another shuttle or otherwise had a credible evacuation plan. The ambush then breaks that prior plan and transfers the problem to the current scene.

This produces a clean causal chain:

**valid prior plan → specific change/failure → old plan no longer works → new pressure or choice**

Avoid:

**unexplained person/problem appears → characters somehow never planned for it → dilemma exists only because the writer withheld logic**

### Cold-read test
Before accepting a scenario, an editor should be able to answer without inventing facts:

- What is happening?
- What does each relevant character know?
- How do they know it?
- Why is the current assumption reasonable?
- What was the prior plan for each important person, resource, obligation, or risk?
- Why was the coming problem not already a problem earlier?
- What changed now?
- What specifically changes when the ambush lands?
- Why does that change pressure, choices, relationships, information, or consequences?

Scenario structures should vary rather than simply changing nouns, and concise prose should still include every fact required to understand the conflict.

## Review pipeline
For Ambush scenario work, use:

**Vale → Skeptic → Professor → Continuity Auditor → Editor → Vale reconciliation**

The Continuity Auditor check is mandatory for new or revised scenarios before they are accepted into the permanent pool.

## Full scenario audit — 2026-10-12

The complete permanent Ambush pool was reviewed using the Continuity Auditor rules.

- **36 scenarios reviewed**
- **20 scenarios repaired for continuity, causal timing, information flow, prior-plan logic, or operational plausibility**
- **16 scenarios passed without substantive continuity repair**
- **0 scenarios remain exempt from the Continuity Auditor check**

Common defects repaired in this pass included:

- information appearing at a convenient moment without a trigger;
- discoveries with no reason to happen during the current scene;
- problems that competent characters should have planned for earlier;
- missing prior plans for people or resources that suddenly become important;
- assumptions that lacked a reasonable factual basis;
- evidence whose source, timing, or meaning was unclear;
- hidden facts that could not plausibly have remained hidden;
- causal chains where the ambush did not actually produce the new problem.

Representative repairs include the evacuation children having confirmed seats on another shuttle until that shuttle is grounded; the salvage-vessel distress beacon becoming reachable only after the salvage terminal creates a communications path; the ceremonial-sword tradition surviving because the shrine was inaccessible for generations; the ancient inscription being uncovered by current altar repairs; and the terraforming dependency being discovered by the first joint restart test.
