Character Filter — Goal and Examples Review

Review method
Vale → Skeptic → Professor → Continuity Auditor → Editor → Vale reconciliation.

Goal
The game is meant to teach character voice through perception. The learner should see that voice is not limited to dialogue or slang: a character becomes distinct through what they notice, compare, value, fear, judge, ignore, and treat as important.

How the mechanic is supposed to meet the goal
A neutral subject is held constant while the learner writes it through three different character filters. Each round also supplies a “voice move” such as comparing the subject to the character’s past, noticing cost, anticipating failure, judging usefulness, fixating on a flaw, or treating a detail as evidence of something larger.

The intended learning effect is contrast. The porch, phone screen, hotel room, family photograph, vending machine, or other ordinary subject should feel different because a different mind is looking at it.

How the examples support the goal
The bank contains 10 neutral subjects, 10 character filters, and 10 voice moves, creating far more than 30 possible combinations. The subjects are deliberately ordinary so the character rather than the event supplies the interest.

The character filters represent different worldviews and priorities: distrust of perfection, nostalgia, efficiency, pessimism, delight in messiness, sensitivity to money, jealousy, status awareness, fear of judgment, and tool-oriented practicality.

The voice moves direct attention toward the kinds of choices that create narrative voice: cost, risk, social interpretation, tiny flaws, usefulness, unfair judgment, evidence of prior use, personal comparison, and symbolic meaning.

Review
The game succeeds at its narrow goal: demonstrating that selection and interpretation create character voice even without dialogue.

There is one important comparison problem. Both the character filter and the voice move change from round to round. If the prose changes, the learner cannot cleanly tell how much of the difference came from the character and how much came from the craft instruction.

For the clearest introductory version, the same voice move should be used for all three characters. A harder version can vary both dimensions. The current random combinations are still generally coherent and useful, so this is a pedagogical-control issue rather than a continuity failure.

A second limitation is scope: the game teaches perceptual voice, not the full range of voice created by syntax, diction, rhythm, metaphor, and sentence structure. The current title and explanation mostly acknowledge that focus.

Verdict
PASS WITH RESERVATION. The combinatorial bank exceeds 30 meaningful exercises, but introductory comparisons would be stronger if the perceptual move stayed constant while only the character changed.
