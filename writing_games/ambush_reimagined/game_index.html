Ambush

Practise escalation by adapting a scene when a credible complication changes its pressure or the characters’ available choices.
Key rule: escalation is not the same as “something bad happens.”An ambush should increase pressure or change the characters’ available choices. It can come from danger, new information, opportunity, exposure, relationship change, increased consequences, or the loss of an option.

Debrief

  • How did the ambush escalate the scene: more pressure, different choices, or both?
  • Which assumption did the ambush attack?
  • What did the characters now have to do differently?
  • What earlier detail made the complication believable?
  • Was the escalation negative, positive-with-a-cost, or simply a change in circumstances?
  • What consequence should carry into the next scene?
Design rule: the complication must grow from the story already in motion and materially change pressure or choices. Escalation does not require disaster; random trouble by itself is not escalation.
